# Portfolio Risk Management

## Risk hierarchy

1. Permanent impairment from weak business economics, leverage, governance, or dilution.
2. Thesis error caused by incorrect operating assumptions or stale evidence.
3. Concentration in correlated revenue and EPS drivers.
4. Liquidity and financing risk under stress.
5. Valuation compression after operating assumptions remain intact.

Price volatility is observable but is not the primary definition of risk in this long-term research system.

## Required controls

- Map each position to one or two quantified downside mechanisms.
- Aggregate shared customer, industry, technology, policy, currency, and funding exposures.
- Set review triggers from operating evidence rather than price movement alone.
- Treat leverage, guarantees, and contingent funding as portfolio-level exposure.
- Preserve the decision and evidence history whenever a limit or thesis condition changes.

## Escalation triggers

Escalate review when a company falsification condition occurs, confidence falls, liquidity deteriorates, a common macro assumption affects multiple companies, or portfolio downside becomes concentrated in one causal mechanism.

## Separation of responsibilities

Company files establish business and earnings truth. Macro thesis files establish shared drivers. This portfolio layer evaluates combined exposure and constraints without rewriting either source.
