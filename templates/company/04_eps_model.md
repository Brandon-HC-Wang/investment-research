# EPS Model

## Model status

State the as-of date, reporting currency, fiscal year-end, latest reported period, forecast horizon, and whether EPS is reported or normalized and basic or diluted.

## Operating assumptions

Maintain an assumption register for units or activity, ASP, product mix, market share, capacity, utilization, FX, and any company-specific drivers. For each assumption, state period, value or range, source or calculation, confidence, and revision date.

## Revenue model

Build revenue from operational drivers by segment. Reconcile segment results to consolidated revenue and explain eliminations or pass-through components.

## Margin model

Model gross margin and operating expenses through utilization, product mix, pricing, input costs, depreciation, headcount, and other material mechanisms. Avoid unsupported margin interpolation.

## EPS bridge

Bridge revenue to gross profit, operating profit, below-the-line items, tax, minority interests, net income attributable to common shareholders, diluted shares, reported EPS, and normalized EPS.

## Normalization

List recurring core, recurring cyclical, project-based, and non-recurring items with pre-tax, tax, net-income, and per-share effects. Explain why each treatment is economically appropriate.

## Scenarios

Maintain downside, base, and upside cases with distinct operating mechanisms. Identify the one or two assumptions driving most of the EPS spread and the evidence that would move the current view among cases.

## Sensitivities

Show EPS sensitivity to continuous variables with material uncertainty, such as ASP, utilization, gross margin, FX, project timing, or diluted shares.

## Assumption history

Append each material forecast revision with date, prior assumption, new assumption, evidence, EPS effect, and thesis implication.

## Model limitations

State missing disclosures, weak proxies, accounting uncertainty, and factors intentionally excluded from the forecast.
