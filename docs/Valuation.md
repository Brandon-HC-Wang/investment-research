# Valuation Policy

Valuation follows business and earnings analysis. It estimates the implications of explicit operating scenarios; it does not determine business quality and is not a recommendation.

## Select methods by economics

| Method | Appropriate when | Key limitations |
|---|---|---|
| P/E | Earnings are positive, representative, and attributable to common shareholders | Distorted by leverage, one-offs, cyclicality, and accounting policy |
| EV/EBITDA | Capital structures differ or depreciation/amortization impairs comparison | Ignores capital expenditure, working capital, tax, and dilution |
| P/B or residual income | Regulated financial or asset-driven returns are anchored to book equity | Book value may not represent economic asset value |
| DCF | Long-term cash generation and reinvestment can be modeled explicitly | Highly sensitive to terminal value and discount rate |
| Sum-of-the-parts | Segments have distinct economics, peers, or ownership structures | Conglomerate costs, tax leakage, and realization discounts matter |
| NAV | Identifiable assets or projects have estimable net values and timing | Sensitive to appraisal, completion cost, tax, and discount timing |

Use at least one primary method and a second method as a reasonableness check when the business supports it.

## Normalize first

Reconcile reported earnings to sustainable earnings before applying a multiple. Adjust for material asset sales, investment gains, fair-value changes, restructuring, PPA amortization when economically justified, abnormal tax, and peak or trough cycle conditions. Show every adjustment; “adjusted EPS” is not self-explanatory.

## Scenario structure

Each valuation should connect to downside, base, and upside operating cases. Scenarios must vary operational assumptions before valuation assumptions.

| Element | Downside | Base | Upside |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue drivers | Explicit adverse mechanism | Most-supported causal path | Explicit favorable mechanism |
| Margins | Consequence of utilization, mix, pricing, or cost | Sustainable central estimate | Evidence-backed operating leverage |
| EPS quality | Recurring and one-off split | Recurring and one-off split | Recurring and one-off split |
| Multiple or discount rate | Reflects quality and risk | Consistent with economics | Requires evidence for improvement |

## Multiple selection

Anchor multiples to sustainable growth, return on incremental capital, cyclicality, balance-sheet risk, governance, and evidence quality. Historical bands and peers are context, not authority. Explain structural differences before comparing companies.

For forward P/E, specify:

- forecast period;
- reported or normalized diluted EPS;
- treatment of net cash, investments, and minorities;
- why the selected multiple fits growth and risk;
- sensitivity to the most uncertain EPS driver.

## DCF discipline

- Forecast revenue through operational drivers.
- Reconcile operating profit to unlevered free cash flow.
- Model reinvestment and working capital explicitly.
- Use a terminal growth rate consistent with economic and industry limits.
- Separate discount-rate sensitivity from operating sensitivity.
- Report the proportion of value from the terminal period; high dependence reduces confidence.

## Asset and project businesses

For construction and development, model projects by attributable ownership, expected completion, revenue recognition, margin, tax, cash collection, and probability. Discount distant projects and do not count land or backlog twice.

## Required disclosures

Every valuation record must state the as-of date, price source if a market comparison is used, currency, share count, net debt or cash, normalized earnings bridge, chosen methods, scenario assumptions, sensitivity, and limitations.

Valuation changes belong in `09_valuation.md`; assumptions that change EPS also belong in `04_eps_model.md` and the timeline.
