# Engineering Metrics

## Order and backlog metrics

| Metric | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| New orders and book-to-bill | Indicates backlog replenishment relative to current revenue |
| Remaining performance obligations | May provide an accounting-based visibility measure |
| Backlog by customer and project | Reveals concentration and execution exposure |
| Cancellation and escalation terms | Determine whether backlog value and margin are protected |
| Expected recognition schedule | Converts backlog into fiscal-year revenue estimates |

## Execution and margin

Track project stage, percentage complete, original bid margin, current estimated margin, cost-to-complete revisions, change orders, liquidated damages, labor and equipment availability, procurement exposure, and customer acceptance.

## Cash and balance sheet

Reconcile profit with advances, receivables, contract assets, retention, payables, provisions, and operating cash flow. Persistent contract-asset growth without collections can indicate aggressive recognition or customer disputes.

## Backlog quality score

Assess enforceability, customer credit, pricing protection, technical complexity, execution capacity, remaining duration, cash terms, and margin evidence. Report gross backlog and risk-adjusted backlog separately when uncertainty is material.

## Warning indicators

Monitor declining book-to-bill, margin revisions, claims treated as income, customer concentration, delayed milestones, cost inflation without escalation, negative project cash flow, and backlog growth beyond delivery capacity.
