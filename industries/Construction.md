# Construction

## Economic model

Construction and property-development earnings are project-based. Reported revenue is shaped by completion and recognition timing, so monthly revenue rarely reveals underlying demand or future EPS on its own.

## Required driver map

Track land bank or contracted work, attributable ownership, total project value, presales, construction progress, expected completion, recognition method, remaining cost, financing, and expected margin. Separate wholly owned, joint-development, urban-renewal, and fee-based construction economics.

## Visibility tests

- Backlog quality: binding terms, cancellations, price escalation, and customer credit.
- Completion realism: permits, contractor availability, funding, and construction progress.
- Recognition: point-in-time versus over-time accounting and handover assumptions.
- Replacement: projects that sustain revenue after current completions.
- Cash conversion: customer deposits, land payments, construction spend, and final collections.

## EPS sensitivities

The largest drivers usually are completion timing, sell-through, gross margin, attributable share, financing cost, and tax. A one-quarter delay can move earnings across fiscal years without changing project economics; distinguish timing risk from value impairment.

## Common errors

- Annualizing a strong completion month.
- Counting gross project value instead of attributable revenue.
- Treating land bank as executable backlog.
- Ignoring capital tied up before recognition.
- Applying a full multiple to disposal gains.
