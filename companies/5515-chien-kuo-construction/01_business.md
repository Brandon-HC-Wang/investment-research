# Business

## Current understanding

Chien Kuo Construction is primarily a contract constructor rather than a property developer. Customers pay it to design, supervise, and build residential, public, industrial, and office projects; substantially all 2026 H1 revenue was construction revenue recognized as performance obligations were satisfied. Customers buy execution capacity, qualification, schedule control, and the ability to deliver complex projects. The company had 19 active projects at 2026-04 and TWD 48.1 billion of then-unrealized contracted revenue, but backlog size does not by itself prove superior profitability. Its large investment portfolio materially changes reported earnings and makes it necessary to analyze construction operations separately from treasury returns.

## Products and customer value

The April 2026 active-project mix comprised four residential projects, eight public projects, and seven industrial or office projects. The contract-value mix was 44% residential, 33% factory/office, and 23% public work. Major disclosed projects included Cathay research/industry and station-front projects, MetaPark, Tainan Spinning's Rende development, several National Housing and Urban Regeneration Center social-housing projects, and the Ministry of Economic Affairs Huaguang project.

Customers principally value tender qualification, cost and schedule execution, safety, subcontractor coordination, and specialist systems integration. Price escalation clauses, performance bonuses, and delay penalties make contract quality and cost control at least as important as the headline award amount.

## Revenue model

Construction revenue can be represented as:

`beginning backlog + new awards - ending backlog = revenue recognized, adjusted for contract changes`

Revenue is recognized over the construction period according to satisfied performance obligations. Margin depends on tender pricing, project mix, progress, labor and material cost, escalation protection, claims, rework, and loss provisions. It is not a monthly unit-sales business; monthly revenue is useful for progress tracking but cannot establish future margin or backlog replacement.

## Cost structure and capital intensity

Subcontracting, labor, materials, equipment, and site overhead dominate construction cost. Working capital is contract-specific: at 2026-06-30, contract assets plus receivables were TWD 1.97 billion, while contract liabilities were TWD 3.19 billion. Positive advance payments currently support liquidity, but project reversals, customer claims, and retention collections can change cash conversion. Reported PP&E is modest; bonding capacity, guarantees, people, and working capital are more binding than owned fixed assets.

## Competitive position and advantage

The evidence supports scale and a broad qualification set, not a proven moat. A 65-year operating history, residential/public/industrial diversification, repeat participation in large projects, and integrated mechanical-electrical capability are candidate advantages. Durability requires evidence that Chien Kuo wins replacement backlog without sacrificing margin and has lower execution losses than peers. No normalized peer comparison or long-run return-on-invested-capital evidence has yet established that advantage.

## Management and governance

Chairman Wu Chang-hsiu and general manager Kan Mao-sheng led the company at the May 2026 investor conference. Disclosure of backlog and project mix is useful, but award additions and margin by project category are not regularly reconciled. The group also holds TWD 5.45 billion of financial assets at fair value through profit or loss; this treasury activity produced more 2026 H1 pretax profit than construction operations, so capital-allocation and risk limits are central governance questions.
