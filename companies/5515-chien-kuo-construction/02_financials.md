# Financials

## Current financial profile

Reporting currency is TWD and fiscal year-end is December. Construction economics improved materially: revenue rose from TWD 7.10 billion in 2025 to TWD 4.02 billion in 2026 H1 (+27.6% year over year), while H1 gross margin reached 19.4% and operating margin 12.6%. Reported H1 EPS of TWD 6.08 substantially overstates sustainable construction earnings because TWD 724 million of fair-value gains and TWD 128 million of land-disposal and compensation gains supplied about 61% of pretax profit. Operating cash inflow was nevertheless positive at TWD 700 million.

August 2026 revenue was TWD 638.0 million, down 8.6% from July and 4.4% year over year. January–August revenue was TWD 5.355 billion, up 23.1% year over year. The cumulative result still supports backlog conversion, but the August slowdown reduced the year-to-date growth rate from 28.1% through July. Monthly revenue does not disclose gross margin or project mix, so it does not change the earnings-quality conclusion by itself.

### 2025 normalized bridge

Reported 2025 EPS of TWD 4.48 included a TWD 289.1 million FVTPL valuation gain. Other gains and losses totaled TWD 266.5 million because the FVTPL gain was partly offset by a TWD 23.0 million foreign-exchange loss and smaller net items; there was no material land-disposal gain comparable with 2026 H1. Removing the FVTPL gain and applying either the reported effective tax rate of approximately 18% or a simplifying 20% tax rate gives indicative 2025 normalized EPS of about TWD 3.3. Interest income and ordinary treasury income remain included because they are recurring consequences of the balance sheet, although they are not construction operating profit.

This means roughly TWD 1.15 per share, or about 26% of reported 2025 EPS, came from the after-tax FVTPL gain. The 2026 base normalized EPS range of TWD 3.8–4.2 therefore represents underlying improvement from an estimated 2025 normalized base near TWD 3.3, not a comparison with the headline TWD 4.48.

## Why fair-value remeasurement enters EPS

Under IFRS 9, an asset classified as fair value through profit or loss (FVTPL) is remeasured at each reporting date, and the change in fair value is recognized in current-period profit or loss even when the asset has not been sold. IAS 33 then uses profit attributable to ordinary equity holders of the parent as the numerator of basic EPS. The accounting path is therefore:

`market value change → other gains and losses → pretax profit → tax → profit attributable to ordinary shareholders → EPS`

This treatment does not mean cash was received. Chien Kuo's 2026 H1 indirect cash-flow reconciliation started from TWD 1,407.1 million of pretax profit and deducted the TWD 724.1 million FVTPL gain as a non-cash adjustment. The contrast is visible in the same filing: the TWD 6.8 million unrealized loss on FVOCI assets was recorded in other comprehensive income and equity, so it did not reduce current-period net income or EPS.

For illustration only, if an FVTPL holding rises from TWD 100 million to TWD 160 million, the company recognizes a TWD 60 million pretax gain without selling it. Assuming a 20% tax rate and 201.6 million weighted-average shares, the estimated EPS contribution is about TWD 0.24: `60 × (1 − 20%) ÷ 201.6`. Actual tax treatment can differ. If the asset subsequently falls, the reversal reduces later EPS; if it is sold at the latest carrying value, the previously recognized appreciation is not recognized a second time.

## Capital allocation

The 2025 earnings distribution approved in 2026 was TWD 3.30 per share. More important than the payout is whether the investment portfolio earns an adequate risk-adjusted return without obscuring construction performance. That judgment remains open.

FVTPL-generated earnings can, in principle, support dividends because they enter net income and then unappropriated retained earnings; there is no separate reserve that automatically quarantines every positive FVTPL unrealized gain. EPS itself is not distributable, however, and consolidated profit is not the same thing as parent-company cash. Before distribution, Chien Kuo's policy and Taiwan Company Act require taxes and accumulated losses to be covered, a 10% legal reserve to be provided until it reaches paid-in capital, applicable special reserves to be provided, and the remaining appropriation to be approved through the corporate process. FSC rules also require a special reserve against net debit balances in other equity, which can reduce distributable earnings but is conceptually different from a positive FVTPL gain recorded in profit or loss.

The 2025 result illustrates the mechanics without identifying the source of each dividend dollar. Reported net income was TWD 903.6 million and included a TWD 289.1 million FVTPL gain; the 2026 appropriation provided TWD 90.5 million legal reserve and TWD 665.3 million cash dividends, or TWD 3.30 per share. The company did not state that a particular portion of the dividend came from FVTPL. An unrealized gain can therefore increase legal accounting capacity to distribute, but paying cash still requires liquid resources; a later market reversal can reduce future retained earnings and dividend capacity.
