# Alltop (3526) Research Report — 2026-11-03

## Executive view

**Opinion:** Alltop has plausible server and automotive content opportunities and positive operating growth. However, H1 2026 revenue growth of 25.03% converted into only 3.66% diluted-EPS growth as margins compressed and potential dilution increased. Sustainable per-share earnings, rather than a reduced share denominator, determine the operating thesis.

**Fact / calculation:** The 2026-10-27 proposal is a 25% cash capital reduction returning TWD 2.5 per old share, subject to shareholder and regulatory procedures. It is not a completed loss-offset reduction. The verified close fell from 257 on 2026-10-27 to 230 on 2026-11-02, or 10.51%; the evidence establishes the price path and net selling, not an isolated causal effect of the proposal.

**Arithmetic boundary:** At an illustrative old price of 257, 1,000 old shares become 750 shares at an unrounded theoretical reference of 339.3333 plus TWD 2,500 cash. The proportional exchange preserves total theoretical value before market movement, rounding, costs and timing. This is not a future price forecast.

**Next verification:** Reconcile Q3 margins, diluted share counts, parent cash and convertible obligations by the internal review target 2026-11-16; verify the prospective 2026-12-17 shareholder decision and subsequent execution terms. This review adopts no numerical FY2026–2028 earnings forecast or target price.

## Business and competitive advantage

### 2026-11-03 initial assessment

**Fact:** Alltop sells connectors and cables for servers, automotive applications and consumer devices. Its product catalog includes high-power and high-speed interconnects. Revenue depends on customer platform shipments, qualified content per platform, supplier share, ASP and currency. It is a component supplier, so customer capital expenditure is not equivalent to its own revenue. [Sources S1–S10](../sources/README.md).

**Interpretation:** Customer value comes from reliable power delivery and signal integrity within thermal, space and qualification constraints. Design qualification, tooling and manufacturing consistency may support retention; neither an AI label nor high historical margins independently proves durable pricing power.

Management describes customization, research investment and Vietnam capacity as competitive tools (S8). Evidence against assuming an unchanging moat is H1 gross-margin compression from 52.07% to 48.92% (S2). Customer-level share, socket continuity and competitor pricing remain unverified.

### Economic engine and durability tests

AI-server power and data complexity can increase connector content even without equal growth in server units. Automotive demand provides another application, but customer pricing pressure can offset shipment growth. Qualification must progress into production and cash collection to create value.

Monitor gross profit in TWD, operating margin, inventory, receivables, content per system and wins across successive platforms. See [growth drivers](../03_growth_drivers.md), [financials](../02_financials.md) and [open questions](../07_open_questions.md).

## EPS bridge and scenario boundaries

### 2026-11-03 initial model boundaries

Revenue = customer platform units × qualified content × supplier share × ASP/mix × FX. Gross profit less operating costs, plus nonoperating items, less tax gives attributable profit; divide by the matching weighted-average basic or diluted share count. [Reported baseline](../02_financials.md); [sources S2–S4](../sources/README.md).

H1 reported profit 598.414 / 65.940 million basic weighted-average shares gives EPS 9.08. The diluted numerator is 608.962 after convertible adjustments of 10.548; the diluted denominator is 76.718 million, including 10.449 million convertible equivalents and 0.329 million employee-compensation equivalents, giving EPS 7.94. Do not simply divide basic profit by the diluted denominator.

### Earnings quality and sensitivity

Start from operating profit; separately model deposit interest, FX, convertible finance costs/valuation, tax and dilution. The H1 financial-instrument gain of 12.301 is not core operating profit. FX losses must not be automatically removed as exceptional. A normalized diluted-EPS point estimate is intentionally withheld until these items are reconciled.

**Sensitivity, not forecast:** On the H1 revenue base, a 1 percentage point gross-margin change, with other costs fixed, changes pretax profit by 23.119 million. At an illustrative 20% tax rate and 65.940 million basic shares, the EPS effect is approximately 0.28. This isolates margin sensitivity and is not a prediction of tax or future EPS.

### Capital reduction mechanics

The proposal implies 67,534,252 old shares and 50,650,689 new shares, subject to subsequent capital changes (S1). In a static full-period example with unchanged profit and no other dilution, EPS scales by 1 / 0.75 = 1.3333. This is denominator arithmetic, not 33.33% operating growth or guidance on reported EPS accounting. Actual timing, weighted averages and convertible terms must be modeled.

Cash leaves the company; interest income can fall. Convertible conversion prices and equivalent shares must be adjusted under each bond's terms, rather than assuming the reduction eliminates dilution. [Q-2026-002](../07_open_questions.md).

## Cash reduction and announcement-period decline

### 2026-11-03 — Why did the stock fall after a cash-reduction proposal?

**Conclusion:** The observed decline is a market repricing before implementation, not the mechanical reduction adjustment. The proposal is not a loss-offset reduction and does not itself prove business deterioration. [Sources S1, S5, S6, S7](../sources/README.md).

TPEx closes were 257 on October 27, 246 on October 28 (-4.28%), 240.5 on October 29, 233 on October 30 and 230 on November 2. The cumulative decline was 10.51%. Prices had already fallen 7.55% from October 6's 278 to October 27's 257. Thus the broader decline began before the proposal; this is not a controlled event study.

On October 28 foreign investors sold a net 309,190 shares and institutions combined 290,435. These are observed flows, not evidence of investors' motives. No broker attribution, benchmark-controlled abnormal return or investor interview was established. No evidence here proves short selling or insider intent.

### Arithmetic that cannot explain the announcement decline

S1 proposes cancellation of 16,883,563 shares and cash return of TWD 168,835,630, or TWD 2.5 per old share. The 25% is a percentage of share capital/share count, not market value. At 257, the cash component is only 0.97% of the share price.

TPEx formula S7: reference price = (last pre-suspension close − cash per old share) / exchange ratio. With an **illustrative** 257 close and 0.75 exchange ratio, the result is 339.3333 before trading-unit rounding. 1,000 old shares worth 257,000 become 750 shares worth 254,500 at that theoretical reference plus 2,500 cash. Ownership percentage is unchanged for a proportional reduction. This is not a forecast or the actual future reference price.

A static EPS rise of 33.33% must be compared with the adjusted price and smaller holding. It does not create 33.33% profit growth. ROE uses total equity, not share capital: the cash return is only 4.08% of June equity, so ROE does not mechanically rise 33.33% either.

### Competing explanations, not proven causes

1. **Growth-expectation hypothesis:** Returning funds may lead investors to question incremental reinvestment opportunities. Against this: the cash return is modest relative to June cash, and management reports new-product and Vietnam investment. Test FY2027 production wins and cash-funded capacity rather than infer demand loss from the proposal.
2. **Earnings-quality hypothesis:** Revenue growth is not converting proportionately into diluted EPS. This is supported by H1 margins and dilution, but those results were already public in August; no new October earnings cut was established. Test Q3 profit, margins and diluted shares.
3. **Trading-flow / liquidity hypothesis:** Existing selling may have amplified the response. Flows support net selling; they do not establish why holders sold or whether future trading interruptions drove decisions. Official implementation dates remain unset.

Implication: distinguish operating earnings growth from share-count engineering. Neither “cash reduction always destroys value” nor “higher EPS guarantees a gain” survives the arithmetic. See [thesis](../08_thesis.md) and [questions](../07_open_questions.md).

## Thesis and operating scenarios

### Current view — 2026-11-03

**Opinion; confidence medium on current economics, low on long-range earnings:** Alltop has plausible server and automotive content opportunities and substantial operating profitability. The evidence supports a growing business, but not an assumption that revenue growth produces equivalent diluted-EPS growth. The reduction is capital allocation, not a substitute for improving operating profit. [Financials](../02_financials.md), [drivers](../03_growth_drivers.md), [event review](../06_notes.md).

No consensus variant perception was verified. A possible misunderstanding to test is that a smaller share count automatically creates shareholder wealth. Value requires durable profit/cash growth or a better allocation of capital, after matching share and price bases.

### Operational scenarios

| Case | Operating mechanism | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|
| Downside | Automotive pricing or platform delays reduce mix/margin; inventories and receivables absorb cash; dilution offsets profit growth | Further margin compression and weak cash conversion alongside revenue growth |
| Base hypothesis | Existing production supports growth; margin stabilizes and new programs replace mature applications | Q3–Q4 profit conversion, orders and funded capacity; no numerical forecast adopted |
| Upside | Higher qualified power/data content and repeat platform wins improve gross-profit growth | Multi-customer production, stable/improving margin and cash after investment |

The two key EPS risks are (1) margin compression from mix, pricing or production costs and (2) weak profit-to-share conversion from convertible dilution, finance/FX effects and cash distribution demands. A 1 pp margin change on H1 revenue is 23.119 million pretax; see [model](../04_eps_model.md).

### Falsification and monitoring

The claim that content growth creates sustainable per-share growth should be challenged if two consecutive reporting periods show growing revenue but declining operating profit or diluted EPS on a comparable share basis. Sustained inventory/receivable growth without cash recovery also challenges earnings quality. These are research thresholds, not company guidance.

Raise confidence only after verified FY2027 production wins, a margin explanation and a parent-level cash/bond funding bridge. Reassess Q3 evidence by 2026-11-16 and the proposed approval event on 2026-12-17. Historical state is retained in [2026Q4](../history/2026Q4.md).

## Valuation method and limits

### 2026-11-03 measurement discipline

Business and earnings assessment precedes valuation: see [business](../01_business.md), [model](../04_eps_model.md) and [thesis](../08_thesis.md). Currency TWD. Latest verified close used here is **2026-11-02, 230**, from TPEx S5; this is not a live quote. No target price is set.

Normalized diluted P/E is the preferred eventual method for a profitable component supplier, with free-cash-flow yield as a cross-check. Durable content, margin, working capital and replacement wins must be established first. A numerical fair-value range is withheld because normalized forward diluted EPS, parent net cash and current fully diluted capitalization are not reconciled. Do not add gross cash to an earnings multiple without netting bond obligations and avoiding double counting interest income.

**Historical arithmetic only:** using FY2025 basic EPS 16.97 gives 230 / 16.97 = 13.55x; diluted EPS 15.42 gives 14.92x. These are FY2025 earnings ratios against a November 2026 price, not current trailing or forward P/E and not matched current fully diluted market capitalizations. June and October share counts differ.

The proposed reduction implies 67,534,252 old shares; June H1 basic/diluted weighted-average counts were 65.940/76.718 million. June convertible liabilities were 2,117.134 million. Current share count, financial-asset availability and bond terms remain under [Q-2026-002](../07_open_questions.md).

### Reduction invariance example

A static pre-reduction EPS of 20 would become 26.6667 at unchanged profit with 25% fewer shares. At an illustrative 257 price, adjusted reference is (257 − 2.5) / 0.75 = 339.3333. Comparing old price with new EPS overstates cheapness. Cash return, lost interest, execution date and adjusted convertible dilution must be included; this is a teaching example, not an EPS forecast.

Downside/base/upside valuation work must first change the operational mechanisms in the thesis, then apply justified multiple or cash-flow assumptions. A reduction alone justifies no higher multiple.

## Testable assumptions

### A-2026-001 — Qualified content can produce sustainable earnings growth

- **Opened:** 2026-11-03. **Status:** Open. **Confidence:** 55%.
- **Claim / mechanism:** Higher server power/data requirements increase qualified content and gross profit, overcoming mature-product replacement and dilution during FY2027–2028.
- **Supporting evidence:** Growing revenue and operating profit; product offerings and management development commentary (S2, S8, S9).
- **Contradicting evidence:** H1 gross-margin decline and only 3.66% diluted-EPS growth; no quantified multiyear backlog verified.
- **Confirmation:** Production wins across customers and generations, stabilizing margins, diluted-EPS and cash growth.
- **Rejection:** Delays/lost content or two reporting periods of declining comparable operating profit/diluted EPS despite revenue growth.
- **Model effect:** Controls forward revenue, margin, reinvestment and sustainable EPS; no numerical forecast adopted.
- **History:** 2026-11-03 Open, 55%; review Q3 evidence by 2026-11-16, then FY2027–2028 production.

### A-2026-002 — Proposed cash return can coexist with adequate funding

- **Opened:** 2026-11-03. **Status:** Open. **Confidence:** 55%.
- **Claim / mechanism:** Returning 168.835630 million does not materially displace required production investment or bond repayment capacity through FY2027.
- **Supporting evidence:** June cash 1,763.194 million and additional financial assets; cash return 9.58% of that cash.
- **Contradicting evidence:** Convertible liabilities 2,117.134 million, H1 distributions above operating cash flow; parent/restricted balances not reconciled.
- **Confirmation:** Q3 parent cash and a bond/working-capital/capex bridge demonstrate funding after distributions.
- **Rejection:** Required investment delays, distressed refinancing or inadequate debt-payment liquidity attributable to distributions.
- **Model effect:** Interest income, reinvestment, liquidity and dilution. No claim that gross cash is unrestricted surplus.
- **History:** 2026-11-03 Open, 55%; next review 2026-11-16 and 2026-12-17.
