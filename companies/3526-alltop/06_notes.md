# Alltop — Research Notes

## 2026-11-03 — Why did the stock fall after a cash-reduction proposal?

**Conclusion:** The observed decline is a market repricing before implementation, not the mechanical reduction adjustment. The proposal is not a loss-offset reduction and does not itself prove business deterioration. [Sources S1, S5, S6, S7](sources/README.md).

TPEx closes were 257 on October 27, 246 on October 28 (-4.28%), 240.5 on October 29, 233 on October 30 and 230 on November 2. The cumulative decline was 10.51%. Prices had already fallen 7.55% from October 6's 278 to October 27's 257. Thus the broader decline began before the proposal; this is not a controlled event study.

On October 28 foreign investors sold a net 309,190 shares and institutions combined 290,435. These are observed flows, not evidence of investors' motives. No broker attribution, benchmark-controlled abnormal return or investor interview was established. No evidence here proves short selling or insider intent.

## Arithmetic that cannot explain the announcement decline

S1 proposes cancellation of 16,883,563 shares and cash return of TWD 168,835,630, or TWD 2.5 per old share. The 25% is a percentage of share capital/share count, not market value. At 257, the cash component is only 0.97% of the share price.

TPEx formula S7: reference price = (last pre-suspension close − cash per old share) / exchange ratio. With an **illustrative** 257 close and 0.75 exchange ratio, the result is 339.3333 before trading-unit rounding. 1,000 old shares worth 257,000 become 750 shares worth 254,500 at that theoretical reference plus 2,500 cash. Ownership percentage is unchanged for a proportional reduction. This is not a forecast or the actual future reference price.

A static EPS rise of 33.33% must be compared with the adjusted price and smaller holding. It does not create 33.33% profit growth. ROE uses total equity, not share capital: the cash return is only 4.08% of June equity, so ROE does not mechanically rise 33.33% either.

## Competing explanations, not proven causes

1. **Growth-expectation hypothesis:** Returning funds may lead investors to question incremental reinvestment opportunities. Against this: the cash return is modest relative to June cash, and management reports new-product and Vietnam investment. Test FY2027 production wins and cash-funded capacity rather than infer demand loss from the proposal.
2. **Earnings-quality hypothesis:** Revenue growth is not converting proportionately into diluted EPS. This is supported by H1 margins and dilution, but those results were already public in August; no new October earnings cut was established. Test Q3 profit, margins and diluted shares.
3. **Trading-flow / liquidity hypothesis:** Existing selling may have amplified the response. Flows support net selling; they do not establish why holders sold or whether future trading interruptions drove decisions. Official implementation dates remain unset.

Implication: distinguish operating earnings growth from share-count engineering. Neither “cash reduction always destroys value” nor “higher EPS guarantees a gain” survives the arithmetic. See [thesis](08_thesis.md) and [questions](07_open_questions.md).
