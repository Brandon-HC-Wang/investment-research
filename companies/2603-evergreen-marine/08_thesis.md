# Investment Thesis

This is not a buy, sell, or hold recommendation.

## Current thesis

Evergreen is a financially resilient, scaled container carrier whose young fleet and OCEAN Alliance network improve its position within the cycle. Current 2026 evidence supports a strong Q3-Q4 earnings rebound, driven by freight rates rather than financial-asset gains. The company can grow liftings and lower unit costs through fleet renewal, but sustainable per-share compounding is unproven because freight rates remain determined by global effective capacity and disruption. The largest dependency is whether the 53-vessel orderbook creates efficiency faster than it creates excess capacity.

## Variant perception

The evidence-based distinction is between **high-quality current earnings** and **sustainable earnings**. Q2 profit is predominantly operating and cash-backed, so dismissing it as an accounting gain is wrong. Conversely, annualizing July-August revenue or Q3 margin into perpetuity is also wrong because elevated rates reflect a cyclical supply-demand and geopolitical environment.

## Evidence for and against

Supporting evidence is in [01_business.md](01_business.md) and [02_financials.md](02_financials.md): scale, alliance coverage, a younger fleet, strong liquidity, and Q2 operating-margin recovery. Contradicting evidence is in [03_growth_drivers.md](03_growth_drivers.md): large industry and company delivery pipelines, high capital commitments, commoditized slots, and possible effective-capacity release if Suez normalizes.

## Scenario thesis

- **Downside:** Q3 captures the current rate peak, but Suez normalization and new capacity lower Q4 revenue and margin. FY2026 EPS is about TWD28, followed by a weaker 2027.
- **Base:** Rates ease gradually; July-August revenue converts at a mid-20% Q3 operating margin and Q4 remains profitable. FY2026 EPS is about TWD37, but normalized mid-cycle EPS is only TWD22-28.
- **Upside:** Disruption and demand keep routes tight through year-end while the newer fleet contains unit costs. FY2026 EPS approaches TWD45. Realization requires monthly revenue near or above TWD50 billion and no broad Suez normalization.

## Principal risks and falsification

The thesis weakens if WCI/SCFI and Evergreen monthly revenue fall for two consecutive months while scheduled capacity continues rising. It is invalidated as a per-share compounding thesis if 2027 deliveries increase debt and depreciation without corresponding liftings, utilization, or unit-cost improvement ([Q-2026-003](07_open_questions.md)). It strengthens if effective rate and liftings remain firm while cost per TEU falls after new deliveries.

## Capital allocation judgment

Liquidity is strong and the 2025 payout was moderate at 50.5% of EPS. However, remaining newbuilding commitments of roughly TWD237 billion exceed cash available after including lease and borrowing obligations. Capital allocation is therefore provisionally adequate, not proven superior. The relevant outcome is future return per TEU and per share, not fleet size alone.
