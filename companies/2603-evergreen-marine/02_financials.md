# Financials

## Current financial profile

Reporting currency is TWD and the fiscal year ends 31 December. 2026H1 revenue declined 2.4% year over year, but the quarterly trajectory turned sharply: Q2 revenue rose 21.6% from Q1 and operating margin recovered to 18.4% from 10.3%. The earnings recovery is operating, not a financial-asset revaluation story. Cash generation is strong, but the apparent net-cash cushion is partly committed to a large newbuilding program.

## Revenue and margin explanation

2025 revenue fell 18.2% from the disruption-supported 2024 level, while operating profit fell 53.7% because rates declined faster than voyage and fleet costs. 2026H1 revenue was only 2.4% below 2025H1, but operating profit was 35.3% lower because Q1 carried weaker effective freight rates. Q2 then recovered as revenue increased TWD18.65 billion year over year and gross margin rose to 22.8% from 21.6%.

July and August 2026 revenue accelerated to TWD48.08 billion and TWD48.99 billion, respectively. The two-month total of TWD97.06 billion already equals 92.3% of all Q2 revenue, supporting a materially stronger Q3 than Q2. This is a revenue signal, not a complete profit forecast: route mix, contract/spot mix, fuel, congestion, and voyage timing still determine margin.

## Earnings quality

2026H1 operating profit was TWD28.31 billion and below-the-line income was a net TWD3.20 billion. The largest positive non-operating items were TWD3.47 billion of interest income and TWD1.94 billion of equity-method profit; finance cost was TWD2.99 billion.

FVTPL was immaterial: net FVTPL gain was only TWD0.000931 billion in 2026H1 and TWD0.000411 billion in Q2. FVOCI equity valuation gains of TWD0.367 billion went to other comprehensive income, not EPS. Reported EPS therefore largely reflects container-shipping operations, with recurring interest income partly offsetting finance costs.

The main normalization issue is not an accounting one-off; it is the freight-rate cycle. Reported 2026 EPS can be cash-backed and still exceed sustainable mid-cycle EPS.

## Cash flow, balance sheet, and commitments

At 2026-06-30, Evergreen had TWD174.75 billion of cash and TWD17.65 billion of amortized-cost financial assets. Borrowings were about TWD105.58 billion including current maturities and bonds, and lease liabilities were TWD96.91 billion. On a pre-lease basis, cash plus deposits exceeded borrowings by about TWD86.8 billion; including lease liabilities, adjusted net debt was about TWD10.1 billion.

2026H1 CFO was TWD38.78 billion. Cash paid for property, plant and equipment was TWD13.85 billion, while TWD46.72 billion of other non-current-asset additions mainly represented newbuilding prepayments. Prepaid equipment reached TWD111.76 billion. Remaining unpaid newbuilding commitments were USD7.454 billion, about TWD237 billion at the 2026-06-30 exchange rate of TWD31.795/USD. Therefore, gross cash should not be treated as fully distributable excess cash.

## Capital allocation

The 2025 earnings distribution was a TWD16 cash dividend per share, or about 50.5% of reported EPS, versus TWD32.5 on 2024 earnings. The dividend is supported by retained earnings and cash, but the board may vary payout with the freight cycle and fleet funding. The 53 newbuildings due during 2026-2030 are the decisive allocation test: newer ships can lower unit cost and emissions, but excessive industry capacity would depress both freight rates and returns on the investment.
