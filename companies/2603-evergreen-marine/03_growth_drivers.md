# Growth Drivers

## Driver map

| Driver | Type | Observable variable | Revenue and margin mechanism | Current evidence | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Effective freight rate | Cyclical | WCI/SCFI, monthly revenue, route rates | Higher rate per TEU has high incremental margin | Drewry WCI was USD4,465/FEU on 2026-09-03; July-August revenue accelerated | medium-high |
| Liftings and fleet deployment | Structural/cyclical | TEU liftings, capacity, utilization | More filled slots increase revenue and fixed-cost absorption | 2025 liftings 10.8m TEU; capacity 1.96m TEU | medium |
| Red Sea and other chokepoint disruption | Cyclical/event | Suez transits, rerouting, congestion | Longer voyages absorb capacity and support rates, but raise fuel and operating cost | Red Sea risk remained elevated; a Hapag-Lloyd test transit does not prove normalization | medium-low |
| Fleet renewal | Structural/project | delivered ships, owned/chartered mix, cost per TEU | New large dual-fuel vessels may lower unit cost | 53 ships due 2026-2030; seven scheduled in 2026 at 2025 year-end | high on delivery plan, low on returns |

## Current drivers

The immediate driver is freight-rate recovery. Drewry's global WCI rose from USD2,212/FEU on 2026-01-22 to USD4,465/FEU on 2026-09-03. Evergreen's revenue followed with a lag: TWD27.52 billion in March, TWD39.14 billion in June, TWD48.08 billion in July, and TWD48.99 billion in August. The company has meaningful exposure to both Transpacific and Asia-Europe rates because North America and Europe represented 67% of 2025 freight revenue.

Volume and capacity are secondary drivers. Liftings grew at a 7.2% CAGR from 2019 to 2025. New vessels can add saleable slots, but only filled capacity at adequate rates creates value.

## Future drivers and replacement

The 53-vessel orderbook includes seven scheduled deliveries in 2026, 23 in 2027, nine in 2028, 12 in 2029, and two in 2030. Nearly half of the orderbook capacity is concentrated above 15,000 TEU. Fuel efficiency and lower maintenance should improve unit economics, but the program is a replacement driver only if scrapping, slow steaming, demand, or route disruption absorbs the new supply.

The OCEAN Alliance extension to at least 2032 supports long-term network breadth. It does not lock in freight rates or utilization, so it improves competitive positioning more than earnings visibility.

## EPS-relevant risks

1. **Disruption premium reverses while capacity arrives.** A durable return to Suez shortens voyages and releases effective capacity. Combined with global deliveries, lower revenue per TEU can compress margin rapidly. Early warnings are falling WCI/SCFI, lower monthly revenue, and delivery growth above liftings.
2. **Newbuild cash burden outruns cycle cash flow.** Remaining newbuilding payments were about TWD237 billion at 2026-06-30 exchange rates. Lower operating cash flow would increase borrowing or reduce dividends. Watch prepaid equipment, debt, interest expense, and payout.
