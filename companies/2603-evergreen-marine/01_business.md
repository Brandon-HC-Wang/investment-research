# Business

## Current understanding

Evergreen Marine earns almost all revenue from international container transportation, with smaller agency, terminal, inland-transport, and related services. Customers are manufacturers, traders, and retailers that buy reliable scheduled capacity across global trade lanes. Revenue is primarily liftings multiplied by effective freight revenue per TEU; freight rates, route mix, capacity utilization, and voyage completion timing therefore dominate results. The defensible capabilities are network scale, alliance coverage, terminal access, and a relatively young fleet, but liner shipping remains a homogeneous, capital-intensive, and highly cyclical service. This is a strong operator in a structurally volatile industry, not a stable compounder.

## Products and customer value

Evergreen Line runs more than 150 service loops across five continents. At 2025-12-31, the group operated 238 container vessels with about 1.96 million TEU of capacity, ranking seventh globally with roughly 5.8% market share. Its 292 offices and agents in 120 countries support booking, documentation, transshipment, inland transport, and local customer service.

The OCEAN Alliance is the main network multiplier. Its 2026 Day 10 product offers 41 weekly East-West loops, more than 520 direct ports, 390 vessels, and about 5.2 million TEU of deployed capacity. The cooperation is extended through at least 2032. It improves frequency and port-pair coverage without Evergreen owning every vessel required for the network.

## Revenue model

The core equation is `freight revenue = liftings (TEU) × effective freight revenue per TEU`. Freight revenue was TWD354.0 billion in 2025, split 39% North America, 28% Europe, 18% Asia, and 15% other routes. Consolidated revenue was TWD379.1 billion, of which the transportation department contributed 99.8%. Revenue is recognized over voyage progress, so a sharp spot-rate move reaches reported revenue with a lag and is blended with contract cargo.

## Cost structure and capital intensity

Material costs include bunker fuel, charter and lease expense, vessel and container depreciation, port and handling charges, crew, inland transport, and maintenance. Operating leverage is high because voyage and fleet costs do not fall in proportion to freight rates. This explains the movement in operating margin from 35% in 2024 to 20% in 2025 and 15% in 2026H1.

Evergreen owns 68% and charters 32% of its vessel count as of 2025-12-31. Ownership lowers dependence on a hot charter market, while chartering preserves some capacity flexibility. The fleet's average age was 8.7 years versus an industry average of 13.5 years cited by the issuer, but maintaining this advantage requires heavy reinvestment.

## Competitive advantage

- **Scale and network density:** 10.8 million TEU of 2025 liftings, up from 7.1 million in 2019, support route frequency and unit-cost absorption. Durability is medium because competitors can also order large vessels and cooperate in alliances.
- **OCEAN Alliance coverage:** shared operations and coordinated port calls improve customer choice and utilization. Durability is medium-high through 2032, but the advantage is shared rather than exclusive.
- **Young, efficient fleet and terminal access:** 43% of vessels were under five years old at 2025 year-end; investments in Singapore and other key terminals may reduce waiting time. Durability depends on realizing returns from the 53-vessel orderbook without creating excess capacity.

## Competitive landscape and governance

The main competitors are MSC, Maersk, CMA CGM, COSCO, Hapag-Lloyd, ONE, and other global liner operators. Container slots are broadly substitutable, so price, coverage, schedule reliability, and equipment availability matter more than brand alone. The top ten operators controlled about 84% of capacity at 2025 year-end, but concentration has not eliminated rate cycles.

Disclosure is detailed enough to identify route mix, fleet, investments, and commitments. Related-party balances and transactions are material and require continued monitoring, although this initial review did not identify a specific value transfer.
