# Business

> Current state: 2026-11-09. All earlier sections retained below describe prior research and are superseded where this dated update differs.

## 2026-11-09 — Current business assessment

**Fact:** Wowprime sells meals through directly operated brands in Taiwan and China. Customer value comes from recognizable cuisine, price tiers, consistent service and convenient sites. Revenue depends on capacity, visits, table turns and average spend. The company website, dated 2026-09-30, reports **374 Taiwan and 108 China stores, 482 in total**, versus the previously recorded 365/101 at 2026-06-30: net increases of 9/7, not gross openings. Selected Taiwan networks are 12 Hotpot 100, TASTy 38, Tokiya 34 and Giguo 35. [S4](sources/2026-11-09-refresh.json).

**Opinion:** Shared procurement, training, brand incubation and member distribution remain plausible advantages, but scale has not yet established superior unit returns. Store counts are period-end capacity, not a measure of mature-store productivity. China contributes 19.29% of 9M revenue but only 7.80% of the Taiwan-plus-China segment profit; its lower margins constrain the value of faster expansion. Registered members remain a marketing asset whose incremental economics are unverified.

**Economic test:** More locations create value only when post-ramp gross profit covers labor, occupancy, recurring closure costs, fit-out and lease cash commitments. Lease liabilities reached TWD 5,142.105 million; a durable moat requires repeatable lease-adjusted returns across cohorts. [S1, pp. 21-22, 50-51](sources/2026-11-09-refresh.json).

## Prior research — preserved through 2026-10-12

## Current understanding

Wowprime operates multi-brand restaurants, primarily in Taiwan and mainland China. Diners pay per visit for meals across price points and cuisines; revenue therefore depends on restaurant count, customer traffic, average spending, and brand mix. As of 2026-06-30, the company reported 365 directly operated restaurants in Taiwan and 101 in China. The proposed advantage is a shared brand-incubation, procurement, operating, and digital-member platform, but its durability remains an open hypothesis because brand-level same-store sales and unit economics are not disclosed.

## Products and customer value

The portfolio spans steak, hot pot, teppanyaki, Japanese, roast duck, pork cutlet, and other formats. At 2026-04-30, the largest disclosed Taiwan networks were 12 Hotpot with 97 restaurants, TASTy with 37, Tokiya with 34, and Giguo with 31; the portfolio also included smaller premium and incubating brands. The customer proposition is occasion- and budget-specific dining with standardized menus and service under recognizable brands. Repeat frequency, active members, and brand-level customer retention are not disclosed.

## Revenue model

Restaurant revenue is principally a function of:

`restaurants × available seats/tables × table turns × occupancy × average spend`

New stores add capacity, while same-store traffic, pricing, menu mix, holidays, and foreign exchange affect comparable revenue. Gift certificates and stored-value obligations can create contract liabilities before restaurant revenue is recognized. Retail food products are an adjacent channel, but their revenue and profit contribution are not separately disclosed.

## Customers and channels

Customers are individual diners reached through directly operated restaurants, delivery or takeaway channels, and the Wowprime app. The company reported 5.83 million app members at 2026-04-30, but did not disclose active users, purchase frequency, acquisition cost, or member revenue. Retail products also reach consumers through convenience stores and warehouse channels. Customer concentration is likely low at the diner level; platform and landlord bargaining exposure has not been quantified.

## Cost structure and capital intensity

Food ingredients are the main variable cost. Labor, rent and lease depreciation, utilities, and restaurant-level overhead are partly fixed, creating operating leverage when same-store sales change. New restaurants require fit-out, equipment, deposits, pre-opening expense, inventory, and lease commitments. At 2026-03-31, right-of-use assets were TWD 4.82 billion and lease liabilities were TWD 4.91 billion, so store expansion must be judged on lease-adjusted unit returns, not store count alone.

## Competitive landscape

Competition includes independent restaurants, local and international chains, department-store food operators, delivery-first concepts, and convenience retail. Restaurants compete on cuisine, location, price, service consistency, food safety, novelty, and reservation convenience. A sourced peer comparison has not yet been completed and is recorded in `07_open_questions.md`.

## Competitive advantage

Three candidate advantages require verification:

1. **Portfolio and operating system:** shared procurement, site selection, training, and brand incubation could lower the cost and failure rate of launching formats. Evidence is scale and repeated brand launches; contradicting evidence would be weak same-store sales, frequent closures, or poor new-store payback.
2. **Customer data and distribution:** 5.83 million registered app members could improve cross-brand traffic and retention. The missing evidence is active-member behavior and incremental economics.
3. **Procurement and supply chain:** consolidated purchasing and central-kitchen capabilities could support consistency and gross margin. The missing evidence is a demonstrated cost or margin advantage against peers.

## Management and governance

The group reports Taiwan and mainland China operating segments and publishes quarterly presentations and financial statements. Disclosure is adequate for consolidated and geographic results but insufficient for brand-level same-store sales, closures, new-store cohorts, and invested returns. Management's 2026 presentation emphasizes vertical integration and cross-brand customer familiarity; these are management claims pending operating verification.
