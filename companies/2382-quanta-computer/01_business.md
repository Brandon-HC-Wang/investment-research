# Business

## Current understanding

Quanta manufactures notebook computers and server/cloud-computing products for global brand and cloud customers. Customers buy its engineering, qualification, procurement, scale manufacturing, and global delivery capability rather than a consumer-facing Quanta brand. Revenue is mainly recognized on product delivery; expensive processors, accelerators, memory, and networking content can materially inflate revenue without equivalent gross profit. Scale, customer qualification, platform engineering, and supply-chain execution are plausible advantages, but customer bargaining power and thin consolidated margins limit pricing power. The 2025 audited filing describes notebooks and server/cloud products as the group's principal businesses.

## Products and customer value

| Offering | Customer value | Revenue and economics |
|---|---|---|
| Notebook ODM | Integrated design, procurement, assembly, quality, and global delivery | Unit volume × configured ASP; mature, concentrated, cyclical, and price competitive |
| Server and cloud systems | Rapid qualification and volume deployment of complex compute platforms | System/rack volume × content × mix; AI accelerators raise bill of materials and reported revenue, while value-added gross profit depends on design and integration content |
| Related peripherals and services | Repair, logistics, and selected storage or electronics capabilities through subsidiaries | Smaller or undisclosed; may support customer retention but is not separately measurable from current disclosures |

## Customers, costs, and competitive position

Direct customers are global computer brands and cloud operators; end users are consumers and data-center operators. The filing does not disclose product revenue, AI-server revenue, system volume, or named-customer concentration, so bargaining power and content per system remain open questions. Major costs are purchased components and assembly labor; capital intensity is moderate relative to semiconductor fabrication, but AI-server growth is highly working-capital intensive. Competitors include Hon Hai, Wistron/Wiwynn, Inventec, and other ODMs.

The defensible capability is not manufacturing capacity alone. It is the combination of co-design, validation across successive platforms, procurement access, production yield, and delivery close to customers. Durability would be confirmed by repeat qualifications across accelerator generations and by gross-profit growth that persists after the initial ramp; it would be weakened by share reallocation, platform delays, or declining gross profit per revenue dollar.

## Management and disclosure

TWSE identifies Barry Lam as chair and C.C. Leung as general manager. Disclosure is adequate for consolidated financials but insufficient for a high-confidence AI-server model because product mix, units, backlog, and customer concentration are not quantified.
