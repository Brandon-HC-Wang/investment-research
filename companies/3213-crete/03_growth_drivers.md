# Growth drivers and visibility

2026-09-24; sources [IR25, AR25, H126, REV](sources/README.md).

## Causal drivers

- Rugged units and platform upgrades: European defense demand reaches Crete through integrators/distributors, qualification and scheduled deliveries. Management said on 2025-12-16 that NATO-related demand justified two additional lines, targeting capacity expansion of more than 100% and completion in 2026 Q2. That was a plan; this review has not verified commissioned capacity, yield or utilization. Doubling capacity does not imply doubling revenue.
- Product mix: stronger rugged shipments alongside relatively flat commercial-computer sales can support blended margins. H1 disclosed product growth supports this direction; monthly system-division revenue includes a different classification and must not be substituted mechanically.
- Replacement drivers through 2027–2028: platform refreshes, customized interfaces, drone ground-control devices and industrial/mining applications could extend deliveries. AI and drone projects have no separately verified revenue or signed order value; no additional speculative business line is added to forecasts.
- Constraints: memory/storage/battery costs, supply continuity, qualification and customer acceptance can delay conversion. A larger inventory is both possible preparation and cash/obsolescence risk.

## Forward tests

The base model assumes 2026 revenue of TWD 3,800 million; given monthly revenue through August, September–December must average about TWD 327 million per month, subject to the TWD 0.369 million filing reconciliation. This is below the July/August average but requires stronger shipments than the weak prior-year final months. It is an assumption, not booked sales. The base requires full-year growth of 31.61%, versus 17.95% through August, and approximately 68.88% growth in September–December against the weak prior-year period. If the recent shipment pace does not persist, the base EPS must be reduced.

For 2027, rugged revenue grows 20% from the modeled 2026 base, commercial revenue about 3%, and total revenue to TWD 4,300 million. Contract prepayments are supportive but provide neither cancellation terms nor a full delivery schedule. The largest risk is that deferred deliveries, materials or acceptance constraints leave inventory high while margins fall. FX and the unexplained tax rate are a second direct EPS risk.

Review September revenue by 2026-10-12 and Q3 statements by 2026-11-16; look for rolling monthly revenue near the base run rate, gross margin at least 34%, collections improving, and documented capacity completion. These are analyst monitoring thresholds, not guidance.

## 2026-09-24 — Customer-channel interpretation

The verified RODA reseller relationship makes European defense procurement a credible demand transmission mechanism. Framework agreements at the channel do not establish Crete's minimum committed volume, margins or delivery schedule. Integration into MilDef may broaden distribution, but may also concentrate purchasing leverage; both are hypotheses. Confirm through Crete's subsequent customer concentration, binding orders and realized margins. No EPS or valuation assumptions change from customer-name evidence alone. ## 2026-09-24 — Conflict attribution (H-2026-001)

- Claim / mechanism: Russia–Ukraine-related European rearmament is a plausible contributor to rugged demand, transmitted through defense budgets, integrator procurement, Crete deliveries and acceptance. This is not a quantified attribution of earnings to a war.
- Supporting evidence: IR25 slide 13 explicitly discusses the Russia–Ukraine war and European/German defense budgets; slide 6 attributes planned capacity expansion to sharply increasing European NATO-related defense orders. RODA's verified reseller role supports the channel. NATO's 2025 Hague declaration paragraphs 2–3 identifies Russia as a long-term threat and establishes a spending path to 2035.
- Limits / contrary evidence: No contract-level conflict attribution or quantified contribution is disclosed. FY25 sales to RODA declined from FY24 despite the conflict. Customer logos do not prove deliveries to a specific battlefield. IR25 discusses Middle Eastern/Israeli spending, but does not establish an Iran-conflict-specific order. Do not infer procurement by Iran from business with Israeli or Western customers.
- Confirmation: Through FY2026–FY2027, verify binding European program orders, rugged deliveries, margin and collections. Rejection: order reductions/cancellations or evidence that growth instead comes from unrelated programs would weaken this attribution.
- Status: Open; medium qualitative confidence for European rearmament, low for an Iran-specific contribution. No numerical war premium or EPS revision.
- Interpretation: A ceasefire need not immediately end multi-year modernization or replenishment; conversely, procurement deferrals and lower utilization can reduce EPS. Component shortages and cost increases can offset demand. [Sources](sources/README.md).
