# Crete — Russia–Ukraine ceasefire risk, 2026-10-22

**Opinion:** A ceasefire alone is insufficient to invalidate the earnings thesis. It can reduce the valuation assigned to defense exposure before orders change. A durable earnings impairment requires evidence of weaker procurement, deliveries, pricing or utilization. No probability, expected stock-price decline or position-size recommendation is assigned.

## Business and durability

Crete sells qualified, customized rugged computers and operates a commercial-PC channel. Environmental testing, interfaces and long support can support retention, but hardware deliveries remain project-based. A computer used in a modernization program is economically different from ammunition consumed in active combat; the share of Crete revenue tied to each end program remains unknown.

**Facts / attributed statements:** Management's 2025-12-16 presentation connects planned expansion to European NATO-related demand (slide 6) and discusses Russia–Ukraine-related budgets (slide 13). RODA is an established route to Central European defense buyers. NATO's 2025-06-25 declaration paragraphs 2–3 sets a spending path to 2035, with at least 3.5% of GDP for core defense and up to 1.5% for broader security-related investment. These policy commitments are neither funded Crete orders nor a guarantee of persistence after a ceasefire. [Source register](../sources/README.md).

## Demand-to-EPS mechanism and visibility

**Hypothesis — H-2026-001 review:** Modernization, replenishment and platform refresh can outlast active fighting. A ceasefire could nevertheless reduce urgent purchases or defer new programs: slower customer procurement → lower rugged deliveries → lower gross profit and utilization → weaker EPS; prepared inventory and slower collections can also constrain cash and dividends. Newly installed capacity would amplify this risk if demand falls before utilization rises. Commissioning and contract cancellation rights remain unverified.

Supporting evidence is the product/customer channel and the multi-year policy framework. Contrary evidence includes the absence of a quantified Crete backlog, customer concentration, and the retained Q3 system-sales decline of 5.49% QoQ. That decline is not evidence of a ceasefire effect. Neither a budget pledge nor a MilDef group contract can be counted automatically as Crete revenue.

Confirm persistence with binding 2027 program schedules, replacement orders, product sales, margins and collections. Reject persistence if disclosed cancellations/deferrals coincide with weaker rugged sales, utilization or cash conversion. Window: Q3/FY2026 disclosures through FY2027. Status remains Open; qualitative European-rearmament confidence remains medium, while conflict-specific earnings attribution remains low. [Q-2026-006 and Q-2026-010](../07_open_questions.md).

## Valuation and portfolio interpretation

Apply valuation only after the earnings bridge. At unchanged EPS, a hypothetical P/E change from 12 to 10 reduces modeled equity value by 16.67%. Combining EPS 10.49 rather than 13.86 with that multiple change reduces illustrative value by 36.95%, using full-precision EPS and an unchanged discount convention. Both multiples are analyst assumptions; this is not an expected market reaction or a revised fair-value range. Financial-investment marks can also affect equity value, without treating FVOCI gains/losses as EPS.

TPEx reports the 2026-10-22 close at TWD 121.00; this is an end-of-day historical observation, not a live quote. It implies 8.73x the retained 2027 base and 11.54x the combined stress EPS. These ratios do not validate either earnings outcome or establish a price floor. Personal allocation additionally requires current portfolio weight, leverage, cash-use timing and acceptable portfolio loss; acquisition cost alone cannot resolve it.
