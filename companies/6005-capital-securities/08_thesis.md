# Investment thesis

2026-10-12. Qualifying coverage; evidence confidence medium, 2027 earnings/cash-payout confidence low. No buy/sell/hold instruction.

Capital offers a diversified securities-service franchise with an earnings floor from fees, financing and associate profits, but direct trading and market activity make earnings materially cyclical. The 20% Capital Investment Trust stake is supportive and growing; it does not turn the company into a pure ETF-fee annuity. Sustained per-share growth requires franchise share and productive capital to outlast exceptional trading profits, while capital retention may prevent high EPS from becoming equivalent cash dividends.

The [base model](04_eps_model.md) estimates diluted EPS 4.69/4.10 for 2026/2027, with cash DPS 1.64/1.44 at a provisional 35% cash ratio, normally paid in 2027/2028. The lower 2027 EPS reflects normalization, not a forecast that ETF fees disappear. Severe annual downturn scenario EPS 0.45 and upside 5.71 in 2027 illustrate trading/volume leverage; neither is mapped mechanically to an index change. No reliable consensus dataset was obtained, so variant perception against consensus is unknown.

## Two material risks

1. Sustained volume/financing contraction plus net trading losses → revenue and operating leverage fall → EPS and collateral/liquidity weaken → lower capital headroom and cash payouts. Early warnings: service revenue, loan balances, net trading, capital ratio and funding concentration.
2. EPS-to-cash allocation gap → required reserves, unrealized-loss/capital retention or another stock-heavy dividend → cash DPS below the assumed 35% ratio. FY2025 cash 0.40 versus stock 1.31 supplies concrete contrary evidence to a high-cash assumption.

## Confirmation and falsification

By 2026-11-16, reconcile Q3 parent earnings/shares and service/trading results; verify September revenue if released. Through FY2027, base annual services near 13,600, loan spreads and associate profit near 900 should support earnings without repeating the entire 2026 disposal gain. Sustained service revenue materially below 10,000 alongside trading losses challenges the base operating case. Capital headroom approaching the company's disclosed 200% benchmark requires a funding/payout reassessment, not a claim of imminent insolvency. A stock-heavy/low-cash decision rejects the payout assumption independently of the EPS case. [Questions](07_open_questions.md).
