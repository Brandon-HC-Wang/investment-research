## Method and observed arithmetic

Use P/E on sustainable parent earnings as the primary lens; cash-flow coverage is the cross-check. A formal DCF or sum-of-parts is premature without payer economics, investment cash requirements and a clean noncore profit bridge. Property and financial assets must not be added to a P/E valuation at gross book value while ignoring their debt and associated earnings.

At 50.30: FY25 basic P/E = 12.51; FY25 diluted P/E = 14.01. The simple trailing basic-EPS sum is 4.02 − 2.12 + 1.91 = 3.81, implying 13.20 times, but varying weighted shares and source inconsistencies mean this is only a shortcut, not a recomputed statutory TTM denominator. TTM parent income is 289.121 million. Issued shares at June end are 77.753 million; subtracting 4.167 million treasury shares gives about 73.586 million. Neither is asserted to be the exact September free float.

Price / 2026 base estimated EPS 3.79 is about 13.28 times; price / base normalized range 3.90–3.98 is about 12.64–12.90 times. These are conditional multiples, not evidence that the shares are cheap. Base 2027 EPS 3.94 requires 5% live growth, 30.3% gross margin and 23% tax despite rising expenses.

## Multiple sensitivity, not target prices

For diagnostic comparison use 10/12/14 times normalized EPS. These are illustrative required-return choices, not observed peer bands: a payout-growth framework P/E = payout × (1+g)/(required return−g), with 75% payout and (11%,3.5%) or (10%,4.5%) return/growth assumptions, spans about 10.35–14.25. Those assumptions themselves require evidence; declining cohorts or poor reinvestment justify lower values. No mechanical multiple from the company's low-earnings past is used.

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 3.90–3.98 | 39.0–39.8 | 46.8–47.8 | 54.6–55.7 |

The range is conditional arithmetic, not a recommended entry/exit range. Downside and upside operating scenarios must change EPS first; multiplying the same earnings by different multiples alone is not scenario analysis.

Illustrative 70–90% payout on normalized 3.90–3.98 gives DPS 2.73–3.58 and yield 5.43–7.12%, before capital-allocation constraints. The payout range is an analyst sensitivity, not company policy or a dividend forecast.

The principal valuation uncertainty is durability of cash earnings and capital distribution, not which historical P/E percentile the price occupies.
