# Business

## Current understanding

**Fact —** Hiyes International Co., Ltd. is listed on the Taiwan Stock Exchange under ticker 2348. The TWSE identifies its principal activities as real-estate agency and brokerage, advertising, management consulting, residential and building development and leasing, and international trade.

The company website describes the core agency service as product planning, integrated marketing, and transaction intermediation for pre-sale homes, completed homes, residual units, offices, land, and parking spaces. This positions Hiyes between property developers and purchasers. The presence of residential development activities means consolidated earnings cannot be analyzed as a pure recurring brokerage business.

## Customer value and revenue model

**Fact —** For developers, Hiyes provides product planning, packaging, marketing, and sales execution. For purchasers, it acts as an information and transaction intermediary. The company’s disclosed service scope indicates that revenue may include brokerage or agency fees as well as development-related property revenue.

**Hypothesis —** Agency economics should be driven primarily by represented project sales value, fee or commission rate, market share, and the fixed cost of sales locations and personnel. Development economics should be driven project by project by attributable ownership, completion, recognition, and margin. Primary filings must be used to confirm segment definitions and revenue-recognition policies before this driver model is accepted.

## Competitive advantage

No durable competitive advantage has yet been established. The relevant hypothesis is that developer relationships, project-planning capability, geographic sales coverage, and demonstrated sell-through may improve repeat mandates. Evidence still needed includes represented sales value, market share, repeat-client concentration, fee stability, and productivity relative to peers.

## Capital intensity

Agency work should require less balance-sheet capital than direct development, while development and investment projects can require land, construction funding, guarantees, and working capital before recognition. Consolidated returns and risk must therefore be separated by activity.
