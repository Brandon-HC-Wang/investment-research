# Growth Drivers

## Driver map

**Hypothesis —** Brokerage revenue is driven by Taiwan property transaction and presale activity, Hiyes’ represented project value and market share, project sell-through, and the effective fee rate. Development revenue is driven by attributable project completions and recognition schedules. Investment-project income is discrete and must be modeled by project rather than embedded in a recurring growth rate.

## Current evidence

TWSE monthly data show material revenue lumpiness, including TWD 1.498 billion in June 2026 after TWD 140 million in April and TWD 170 million in May. The data establish timing concentration but do not identify whether it arose from brokerage activity, development recognition, or another item. Monthly revenue is therefore a prompt for project reconciliation, not a growth conclusion.

## Backlog and replacement drivers

The 2025-11-25 investor presentation provides a management schedule for completion-method projects and discloses total sales value, participation, and construction-financing limits as of 2025-09-30. Multiplying disclosed total value by participation gives a **maximum economic exposure**, not recognized revenue or profit.

| Scheduled year | Projects included in management schedule | Total sales value × disclosed participation (TWD bn) | Interpretation |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 2026 | Dunyang, A3 Spotlight, Smile Hiyes 2, Dali Yecui, Taiwan Yinfu, Dali Future City | 8.33 | Upper-bound attributable project value before sell-through, handover, costs, tax, minorities, and timing |
| 2027 | Meide City, Nanyue Xiyuan, Nanyue Letian, New HSR, Dali World Study | 9.84 | Same limitation; schedule is management expectation rather than verified recognition |

**Fact —** Hiyes stated through July 2026 reporting that Dunyang, A3 Spotlight, and Dali Future City had begun handover in June and contributed TWD 1.255 billion of construction revenue that month. Management also expected Smile Hiyes 2, Dali Yecui, and Taiwan Yinfu to contribute before year-end. This confirms the direction of the 2026 schedule but not full-year sell-through, attributable profit, or margin.

## 2026 Q2 filing implication

**Fact —** The Q2 statement reports TWD 1.255 billion of standalone property-sale revenue, compared with TWD 704 million in Q1. Using the H1 segment note less the Q1 filing, construction segment profit rose from approximately TWD 6 million in Q1 to approximately TWD 298 million in Q2. This confirms a handover-driven construction recovery in Q2, but the filing does not identify revenue, gross profit, sold units, or cash collection by named project.

**Fact —** The replacement engine is not yet visible in the agency stream. H1 service revenue fell 44.6% year over year to TWD 1.157 billion. Inventory nevertheless increased TWD 2.599 billion from 2025 year-end to TWD 28.210 billion, including major increases in the Tainan Pingshi, New Taipei Xinzhi, and Kaohsiung Houbitian balances as projects moved among construction and completed inventory categories.

**Interpretation —** 2026 project timing is more credible than it was before the filing, but cumulative project economics and 2027 replacement remain unverified. The next evidence must reconcile the six scheduled 2026 projects to H2 handovers, project-level margin, cash collection, and debt reduction. Revenue recognition without inventory conversion and deleveraging would not establish sustainable per-share growth.

**Estimate —** The preliminary EPS scenarios recognize 60%–70% of the 2026 upper-bound project value in the conservative range and 85%–95% in the optimistic range. For 2027, the ranges are 35%–45% and 65%–75%, respectively, because completion dates and sold units are less verified. The schedule must be replaced with actual permits, completion, sales, and handover evidence when available.

## EPS-relevant risks

1. **Recognition gap:** if large 2023–2025 project contributions lack replacement projects, normalized EPS could fall even if brokerage operations remain profitable. The early warning is a thin attributable completion schedule for 2027–2028.
2. **Transaction-volume operating leverage:** lower property transactions may reduce agency fees faster than sales-personnel and site costs can adjust. The early warning is weaker represented sales value combined with lower segment margin.
