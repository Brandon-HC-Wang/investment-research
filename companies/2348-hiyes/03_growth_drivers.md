# Growth Drivers

## Driver map

**Hypothesis —** Brokerage revenue is driven by Taiwan property transaction and presale activity, Hiyes’ represented project value and market share, project sell-through, and the effective fee rate. Development revenue is driven by attributable project completions and recognition schedules. Investment-project income is discrete and must be modeled by project rather than embedded in a recurring growth rate.

## Current evidence

TWSE monthly data show material revenue lumpiness, including TWD 1.498 billion in June 2026 after TWD 140 million in April and TWD 170 million in May. The data establish timing concentration but do not identify whether it arose from brokerage activity, development recognition, or another item. Monthly revenue is therefore a prompt for project reconciliation, not a growth conclusion.

## Backlog and replacement drivers

No verified attributable backlog, unrecognized project table, or 2026–2028 completion schedule has yet been assembled. This is the binding evidence gap for future EPS visibility. The next research cycle must separate represented agency projects from owned or invested projects and avoid counting gross project sales as attributable company economics.

## EPS-relevant risks

1. **Recognition gap:** if large 2023–2025 project contributions lack replacement projects, normalized EPS could fall even if brokerage operations remain profitable. The early warning is a thin attributable completion schedule for 2027–2028.
2. **Transaction-volume operating leverage:** lower property transactions may reduce agency fees faster than sales-personnel and site costs can adjust. The early warning is weaker represented sales value combined with lower segment margin.
