# Metalens

## Definition

A metalens uses engineered subwavelength structures to shape light in a thin optical element. Potential benefits include reduced optical thickness, integration, and new sensing functions, but commercial value depends on performance, manufacturability, yield, cost, qualification, and system-level benefit.

## Commercialization ladder

`laboratory result → prototype → customer sampling → qualification → design win → production order → recognized revenue → repeat platform adoption`

Do not treat early technical announcements as forecastable earnings. Record aperture, wavelength, efficiency, image quality, polarization dependence, materials, manufacturing process, wafer or substrate size, yield, packaging, customer qualification, and competing optical designs.

## Earnings bridge

`addressable devices × adoption rate × content per device × supplier share × ASP → revenue`

Margin then depends on yield, process steps, substrate cost, packaging, intellectual-property economics, and scale. Replacement visibility requires adoption across more than one customer or device generation.

## Key uncertainties

- Performance relative to refractive optics under real operating conditions.
- Mass-production yield and cost at the required aperture.
- Customer qualification and redesign cost.
- Whether system simplification creates enough value to support price and margin.
- Competing technologies and intellectual-property constraints.
