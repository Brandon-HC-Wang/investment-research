# DRAM Cycle

## Definition

The DRAM cycle reflects the interaction of bit demand, content per device, supplier capacity and process migration, inventory, product mix, and contract or spot pricing. Revenue and EPS can change sharply because price and utilization affect margin as well as sales.

## Causal model

`end-device demand × DRAM content → bit demand`

`wafer capacity × process productivity × yield → bit supply`

The balance between bit demand, supply, and inventory influences pricing; pricing, mix, utilization, depreciation, and cost per bit determine gross margin and EPS.

## Required evidence

Track supplier and customer inventory, bit shipments, bit growth, ASP, contract and spot prices, utilization, capital expenditure, technology migration, yield, cost per bit, product mix, and end-market demand. Separate commodity DRAM from structurally different products when economics diverge.

## Common analytical errors

- Using spot price as the company’s realized ASP.
- Treating price recovery as structural volume growth.
- Ignoring process-migration supply growth.
- Extrapolating peak margins without capacity response.
- Combining conventional DRAM and higher-value memory without a mix bridge.
