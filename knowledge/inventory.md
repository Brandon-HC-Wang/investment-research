# Inventory

## Definition

Inventory is capital committed to goods or work awaiting sale, processing, completion, or recognition. Its interpretation depends on the value chain: semiconductor work in process, distributor stock, finished devices, property under development, and construction contract assets have different economics.

## Causal role

`demand expectation → production or acquisition → inventory → shipment or completion → revenue → provision or reversal → cash conversion`

Inventory growth can support future sales, signal weak demand, reflect strategic stocking, or arise from cost inflation. The balance alone does not identify the mechanism.

## Analysis protocol

Track value, units when available, days, composition, location in the channel, aging, accounting cost method, write-down policy, purchase commitments, lead times, and subsequent sell-through. Compare inventory with revenue, utilization, ASP, orders, payables, and cash flow.

## Common analytical errors

- Treating company inventory as total channel inventory.
- Interpreting higher days without considering price or mix.
- Ignoring inventory provisions and later reversals in normalized margin.
- Assuming restocking equals end-demand growth.
- Ignoring purchase commitments and cancellation exposure.
