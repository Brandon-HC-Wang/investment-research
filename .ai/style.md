# Research Writing Style

## Voice

Write like a disciplined buy-side analyst: direct, neutral, skeptical, and specific. Confidence should come from evidence, not tone.

## Structure

- Lead with the analytical conclusion, then give evidence and uncertainty.
- Keep one claim per paragraph.
- Use headings that describe content rather than generic labels.
- Use tables for comparable periods, scenarios, projects, or driver bridges.
- Use bullets for discrete items, not as a substitute for causal reasoning.
- Keep the standard company headings intact so tools and agents can navigate reliably.

## Claim labels

Use these labels where readers could confuse evidence with interpretation:

- **Fact —** directly supported by a cited source.
- **Estimate —** calculated from facts and stated assumptions.
- **Hypothesis —** testable claim awaiting sufficient evidence.
- **Opinion —** judgment based on identified evidence.

## Numbers and dates

- Use ISO dates: `2026-08-04`.
- Identify fiscal versus calendar periods.
- State currency and scale in table headings, such as `TWD million`.
- State whether EPS is basic or diluted and whether figures are reported or adjusted.
- Keep decimal precision consistent with source quality.
- Use `–` for unavailable data and explain material gaps; do not use zero for missing data.

## Sources

Prefer compact source references in this form:

> Company or publisher, “Document title,” publication date, page or section, URL, accessed YYYY-MM-DD.

Summarize sources in original language. Do not paste lengthy disclosures. When sources conflict, record both, assess their relative reliability, and keep the conflict open until resolved.

## Analytical language

Prefer:

- “Revenue increased because unit shipments rose 18%, partly offset by lower ASP.”
- “The estimate assumes utilization reaches 80% by FY2027.”
- “This would reduce base-case EPS by approximately 12%.”

Avoid:

- “Revenue performed well.”
- “The outlook is very exciting.”
- “The stock is cheap.”
- “AI is a catalyst” without timing, exposure, and earnings mechanics.

## Uncertainty

State what is unknown, why it matters, the range of plausible outcomes, and the evidence required to narrow the range. Do not conceal uncertainty behind a single precise forecast.
