# AI Server Thesis

## Industry

AI compute infrastructure spans accelerators, server and rack integration, networking, memory, storage, power, cooling, interconnects, and contract manufacturing. Economics vary by value-chain role and by whether expensive components are value-added or passed through.

## Current stage

- **Thesis status:** Open.
- **Research stage:** Initial causal framework established; the repository has not yet completed a dated primary-source review sufficient to label the current deployment stage.
- **Confidence:** Low until deployments, content, margin, and replacement roadmaps are reconciled.

## Working thesis

AI infrastructure can create multi-year earnings growth for suppliers whose content per deployed system rises, qualifications persist across platform generations, and incremental gross profit converts to cash. Revenue growth alone is insufficient when accelerator or memory pass-through depresses margin and consumes working capital.

## Key companies

The observation set should cover accelerator designers, foundries, networking vendors, memory suppliers, server OEMs, original design manufacturers, and power or thermal suppliers. Initial reference companies include NVIDIA, AMD, Broadcom, TSMC, Samsung Electronics, SK hynix, Micron, Dell, HPE, Super Micro, Quanta, and Wiwynn. Inclusion identifies a value-chain role, not an investment conclusion.

## Risks

- Deployment digestion after capacity is ordered ahead of power, networking, or end-workload readiness.
- Customer concentration and bargaining power reducing supplier margins.
- Platform transitions displacing prior content or requiring costly requalification.
- Pass-through revenue overstating value creation and cash conversion.

## Future catalysts

- Production ramps for successive accelerator and rack architectures.
- Greater power and cooling content per rack.
- Broader inference deployment and customer diversification.
- Evidence that content and design positions persist beyond one platform.

## Verification

Confirm through deployed systems, company content, qualification stage, gross profit dollars, working capital, cash flow, and replacement platforms. Reject the thesis for a supplier when growth depends on a single temporary ramp without durable content or acceptable incremental returns.
